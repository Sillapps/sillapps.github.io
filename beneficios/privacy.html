What the app is

The app shows the payment dates of social benefits in Brazil, Mexico and South Africa from the official calendars published by each government. It is informational and unofficial: it does not represent any government entity and is not affiliated with any government or agency.

Data you enter, kept on your phone

The app never asks for a CPF, NIS, CURP, ID number, card, bank account, password or any document number. It only needs the one thing the official calendar uses: the last digit of a number, a birth month, the first letter of a surname or a grant group, plus an optional nickname for each person you follow.

These choices, your country, alert preferences, Pro and trial status, local usage counters (first day of use, days opened, when a reminder or rating prompt was shown) and a short local log of unexpected errors are stored only on your device. They are not sent to the developer.

Android backup: if backup is on in your phone settings, Android may copy these settings to your Google account so they come back after a reinstall. This is handled by Google under your account settings. On iPhone, app data is part of your device backup (iCloud or computer) like any app's, under your control and Apple's policies; the developer has no access to it.

Network requests

The app downloads the payment calendars from https://sillapps.com/beneficios/calendars.json, a static file hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web request, it reveals your IP address to the host (GitHub, Inc., USA), which may keep technical logs for security. The request carries no identifier or data about you. Links to official websites open in your browser.

The free version shows one banner ad on the main screen (not on the first day of use; never in notifications, the widget or the purchase screen). Ads are served by Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK). Google may collect and share the device advertising ID, approximate location derived from the IP address, app interaction data and diagnostics, to serve and measure ads, for analytics and to prevent fraud (see https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), the app asks for your consent with Google's User Messaging Platform before ads are requested, and Settings > Ad privacy choices lets you change it. Elsewhere, ads are shown on the basis of the developer's legitimate interest in funding a free app; you can limit personalized ads and reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings. On iPhone, the app never asks to track you and ads are served without the advertising identifier (IDFA). Buying Pro removes ads.

The app only asks the store whether Pro is owned. On Android, the free trial is stored on your device and needs no account or card; on iPhone, it is a free App Store purchase (nothing is charged), so the App Store remembers when it started.

Notifications and widget

Payment alerts are scheduled locally on your device and the home screen widget reads dates stored locally by the app. Neither sends data anywhere.

New dates alert (Pro or free trial): when your alerts are on, the app registers with Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (and, on iPhone, Apple's push notification service), which gives your phone a push token, and subscribes it to one topic per program you follow (for example Bolsa Família), shared by everyone who follows that program. When an official calendar adds payment dates, an automated job sends one message to that topic. The message is the same for everyone and contains no personal data: your phone then downloads the calendars and works out your own dates. The developer never receives the tokens and keeps no list of users. Google stores the token and its topics to deliver the messages, as a processor, under the Firebase terms (https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy), so Google can tell which programs' topics a token follows, not who you are; it also sees your IP address when your phone connects. Turning the alerts off, or the end of the free trial, unsubscribes your phone and deletes its token. If you never turn the alerts on, no push token is created.
